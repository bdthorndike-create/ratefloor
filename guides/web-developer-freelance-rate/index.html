Freelance Web Developer Rate Guide

The U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics reports a median annual pay of $99,520 for web developers and digital designers (May 2025 data). The BLS figure covers web developers and digital designers together.

Why that number is not your freelance rate

Spread across a standard 2,080-hour paid year, $99,520 works out to about $47.85 per hour (my arithmetic, not a BLS figure). An employee's employer also typically pays for benefits, equipment and unbillable time. As a freelancer you pay for all of that yourself, plus self-employment taxes, and you only earn for the hours you actually bill. That is why a freelance rate that matches an employee's pay has to be noticeably higher than the hourly equivalent.

What to add to your rate

Developers often add costs a salary hides: hosting and tooling subscriptions, a reliable computer, and time spent on proposals, scoping and client communication. Fixed-price projects also carry the risk of scope creep, so many freelancers price in a buffer.

Try your own numbers

Open the calculator with $99,520 as a take-home goal. The other inputs are example values, so replace them with your own tax rate, insurance, costs, time off and billable share.

Source: U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics, Occupational Outlook Handbook, web developers and digital designers. Median pay is for employees across the United States and varies by location, experience and employer. This is general information, not tax or financial advice.
